## What I worked on

October is where the log fills in: 30 problems solved over 15 active days, and one more attempted without a solve. From 5 to 14 October I practised every day, my longest streak so far.

The first week was 800-rated implementation: 50A, 617A, 791A, 977A, 231A, 116A, 467A and 677A, then string problems such as 281A, 59A and 734A. From 10 October more of the problems needed sorting (405A, 236A, 160A, 1399A), and the ratings went up. I reached 1000 with 1A, 1100 with 1791E and 706B, and 1200 with 1790D. On 13 October I solved my first 1300, 4C Registration System, using a map. The day before, 263A Beautiful Matrix was my first 2D grid problem.

Not all of it was independent. 158A Next Round needed a few small hints. 1791D Distinct Split is an assisted attempt: I looked up the two-map sweep, so it does not count as solved. I also redid three problems: 69A, 339A and 2275B, which I upsolved on 7 October and rewrote from scratch on the 11th.

## What the notes say

Some mistakes repeat. My notes for 69A and 1791E both warn that `+=` means "add to" and `=+` is something else, and the 1791E note calls it a recurring bug. In 59A I made the decision inside the counting loop before the count was finished. The fix was to count everything first, then decide.

The notes also record ideas I want to keep:

- 405A: sometimes a "simulation" is just a sort in disguise.
- 1790D: the maximum frequency is only a lower bound, because gaps force extra sets. The lesson I wrote down was to try to break a hypothesis with a tiny counterexample before coding it.
- 1791E: the parity of the number of negatives stays the same. The same note says nested loops are not automatically O(n^2). Count how many times the innermost line runs in total.
- 706B: brute force is about 1e10 operations, so sort once and binary search for the first price above m.
- 1A: use long long, because n and m go up to 1e9 and the answer can reach 1e18.

## What comes next

On the roadmap, October covered fundamentals, maths, and sorting and greedy. The stage after sorting and greedy is prefix sums and two pointers: turning an O(n^2) pair or subarray search into a single pass. Apart from the log note on 706B, nothing I've solved covers it yet.

My notes for 1791D say to redo it from scratch. A practice session on 20 October is recorded only in a commit message, so the log treats it as unverified, and 1791D still counts as not solved.
